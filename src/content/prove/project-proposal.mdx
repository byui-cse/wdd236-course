## Overview

In this assignment you will produce a planning package for a web application idea. The focus is on UX/UI principles, wireframing, and information architecture.

You will start with a constrained project choice and then work through an artifact ladder. This gives you a repeatable process now and prepares you to apply the same planning approach again for your capstone idea later in the semester.

Do not think about implementation details yet. Focus on the user problem, structure, and interaction flow.

This prove has two expectations:

1. Consolidate your best work from Unit 1 prepare and ponder activities.
2. Extend that work into a more complete and cohesive planning package.

## Objectives

- Define a clear user problem and audience
- Apply core information architecture principles to a real concept
- Create low-fidelity UI artifacts for a critical workflow
- Document design decisions and tradeoffs in a way teammates can use

## **01** Constrained Choice

Choose one concept from the instructor-provided project list.

Starter project ideas:

1. Campus Event Planner
   - Students browse events, RSVP, and save events to a personal schedule.
2. Study Group Finder
   - Users create study sessions, join groups, and coordinate meeting details.
3. Local Service Marketplace
   - Users post small jobs (tutoring, yard work, moving help) and others can claim them.
4. Habit and Goal Tracker
   - Users define goals, log progress, and view simple weekly streak summaries.
5. Club and Team Hub
   - Clubs publish announcements, track members, and manage event sign-ups.
6. Recipe and Meal Planner
   - Users save recipes, build weekly meal plans, and generate a shopping list.

If you want to use a different concept, get instructor approval before continuing.

>The project you choose here will not necessarily be the project idea you use for the final project at the end of the semester. The goal is to practice the planning process on a constrained concept before applying it to your own idea later.

For your chosen concept, provide:

1. Problem statement (2-4 sentences)
2. Target users and context
3. MVP boundaries:
   - In scope
   - Out of scope
4. One primary persona (name/role, goal, pain point, context)
5. Two user stories tied to that persona
6. Success criteria (how you will know this solves the problem)

## **02** Artifact Ladder

Create the following artifacts in order. Keep them concise and centered on one critical workflow.

### Step A: Critical Workflow Definition

Define one high-value user task from start to finish.

This workflow should map directly to one of your user stories.

Include:

1. Trigger (what starts the workflow)
2. Main path (key steps)
3. One alternate or error path (and recovery)
4. End state (what counts as success)

### Step B: Information Architecture

Create a first-pass sitemap that supports the workflow above.

Include:

1. Primary views/pages
2. Navigation relationships
3. Where the critical workflow is located in the structure
4. One IA risk or uncertainty to discuss with your team

### Step C: Low-Fidelity Wireframes

Create low-fidelity wireframes for the critical workflow.

Minimum requirement:

1. At least 3 screens or states
2. Clear labels for key actions and content regions
3. One short annotation per screen explaining intent
4. One accessibility note for the workflow (for example: focus order, clear labels, or error messaging)

### Step D: Decision Notes

Add a short section with:

1. Three design decisions you made
2. One tradeoff you accepted
3. Two open questions to bring to team discussion

>Your artifacts are expected to change over time. That is part of the process. The goal is to create a strong first version before coding begins.

## **03** Synthesis and Extension Requirements

Your final submission should not only repeat your weekly drafts. It should revise and expand them.

Minimum expansion requirements:

1. User stories:
   - Include at least 6 total stories.
   - Group them as Must Have, Should Have, and Could Have.
   - Add acceptance criteria for at least 2 Must Have stories.
2. Information architecture:
   - Update your sitemap to include all pages needed for your Must Have stories.
   - Clearly mark new pages or flows added after week 1.
3. Wireframes:
   - Include wireframes for newly added Must Have views/pages.
   - Submit at least 5 total screens/states across your critical workflows.
4. Coherence check:
   - Show how persona -> stories -> workflows -> sitemap -> wireframes connect.

## **04** AI Support Guidance

AI is encouraged for brainstorming and drafting, but you are responsible for reviewing and improving everything you submit.

If you used AI, include a short note describing:

1. Which sections were AI-assisted
2. What you changed after review
3. What still needs validation from peers or users

## **05** Unit Timeline and Milestones

This prove submission is due at the end of Unit 1 and should reflect learning from both weeks.

Week 1 milestone (problem framing):

1. Constrained concept selected
2. Problem statement drafted
3. MVP boundaries drafted
4. Primary persona and user stories drafted

Week 2 milestone (artifact quality):

1. Critical workflow drafted from one user story
2. Sitemap and IA risk note completed
3. Low-fidelity wireframes completed
4. Accessibility annotation and decision notes completed

Final prove expectation:

1. Integrate and polish both weeks of work.
2. Expand beyond drafts to a complete, instructor-ready proposal.

## **06** Submit

Submit one planning package (document, slide deck, or board link) that includes all required sections from Steps A-D and the synthesis/extension requirements.

Your package should be clear enough that another student could understand the user problem and planned workflow without a live explanation.

## **07** Rubric

Use this rubric to self-check before submitting.

| Criteria | Exceeds | Meets | Needs Revision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Constrained Choice and Scope | Problem statement is specific, user-centered, and includes clear MVP boundaries, persona/story alignment, and measurable success criteria. | Problem statement, users, MVP boundaries, persona, stories, and success criteria are present and understandable. | Scope is vague, missing key sections, or success criteria are unclear. |
| Story Coverage and Prioritization | Includes 6+ meaningful stories with clear prioritization and strong acceptance criteria for key Must Have stories. | Includes at least 6 stories, priority grouping, and acceptance criteria for at least 2 Must Have stories. | Story set is incomplete, unprioritized, or missing acceptance criteria. |
| Critical Workflow Definition | Main path and alternate path are realistic, complete, and easy to follow end to end. | Trigger, main path, alternate path, and success state are present with reasonable clarity. | Workflow is incomplete, ambiguous, or missing alternate/error handling. |
| Information Architecture | Sitemap fully supports Must Have stories, clearly marks new pages/flows, and shows strong navigation logic. | Sitemap includes key pages/relationships for Must Have stories and identifies new additions and IA risk. | Sitemap is incomplete, disconnected from Must Have stories, or missing new-page/flow clarity. |
| Wireframes | Five or more low-fidelity screens are clear, annotated, and cover critical Must Have workflow states with accessibility intent. | At least five screens/states are included with labels, annotations, and one accessibility note. | Too few screens, weak labeling/annotations, or missing accessibility note. |
| Decision Notes and Reflection | Decisions and tradeoffs show strong reasoning and open questions are actionable for team discussion. | Three decisions, one tradeoff, and two open questions are included and understandable. | Notes are superficial, incomplete, or missing required elements. |
