# Peer Contract Review

## Purpose

A peer contract review is a two-way conversation about the slices in one learning contract. You explain your work, your partner asks questions, and together you find gaps and connections. Your peer does not certify anything. The review prepares you for the [instructor or TA competency pass-off](../competency-pass-off-new).

> Record each review in your [Competency Progress Tracker](../competency-progress-tracker.md).

## This is a conversation, not a presentation

The review works best when both people are actively thinking. Your role as reviewer depends on how well you know the material.

**If you know the slices well (higher mastery):**

- Ask questions that help your partner connect the dots, not questions that give away the answer.
- Ask "why?" and "what would happen if...?" instead of correcting.
- Let your partner finish thinking before you add anything.

**If you are newer to the slices (lower mastery):**

- Keep asking questions until you understand it yourself.
- Ask your partner to show it again, use a different example, or explain a term.
- Your confusion is useful. If your partner cannot explain it simply, that points to a gap worth reviewing.

Either way, both of you should leave understanding the slices better than when you started. If only one person is talking, change the format: ask a question or switch roles.

## Before you meet

- [ ] List the slices in your contract.
- [ ] Revisit your contract's guiding question and identify what you can now explain and what remains uncertain.
- [ ] Make sure your work runs and you can show it.
- [ ] Gather at least one piece of evidence for each slice (a test, screenshot, inspection result, or note).
- [ ] Challenge your work using the guidance below and bring brief investigation notes.
- [ ] Fill in the top of the [review record](#review-record).

Do not memorize a script. You should understand the work well enough to explain it in your own words.

### Try to break your work

**How many ways did you find to break what you did this week, and how did you make it more stable?**

Choose meaningful challenges related to your competency slices. For example, try a missing attribute or very long content for a component, a failing assertion for a test, keyboard-only use for an interaction, or empty data and failed requests for an async feature. For blueprints, ask a peer to describe what they would build from your documents without extra explanation.

Before each check, record your own prediction. Test it, compare the result with your prediction, and investigate unexpected behavior using evidence. If you make a change, repeat the check and verify that the normal behavior still works. Apply what you learned to another relevant variation. Bring brief notes or a demonstration to discuss with your peer.

The number of failures is not the measure of success. You do not need to introduce defects or find a bug in every check. A successful check is useful evidence when you can explain what it tested and its limits. Record unresolved findings so they can guide your next step or contract.

For each investigation, capture: **What did I challenge? What did I predict? What happened? What did I change or learn? How did I verify it? What remains uncertain?** AI may suggest challenges; your predictions and observations should reflect your own thinking and the checks you actually performed.

## The conversation

Work through these steps for every slice in the contract.

1. **State the goal.** What did you intend to learn or be able to do, and how does it connect to your project and guiding question?
2. **Show it working.** Demonstrate a normal case and, when relevant, an edge case (empty data, invalid input, missing attribute, failed request).
3. **Explain the learning.** Show how you challenged the work. What did you predict, what happened, and what evidence explains the result? What became more stable, how did you verify it, and what remains uncertain?
4. **Ask and answer questions.** The reviewer chooses questions from the [question bank](#question-bank). Follow up on answers, and swap roles if the reviewer has something to demonstrate too.
5. **Summarize.** Write down one thing to review and one thing you are ready to demonstrate at the pass-off.

## Question bank

Choose a few questions that fit the slice. You do not need to ask them all. Follow-up questions based on what you hear are often better than these.

### Any slice

- What were you trying to learn or improve?
- What part is hardest to explain?
- What choice had more than one reasonable option?
- What evidence shows your solution works?
- What did you try when you got stuck?
- What would break first if the requirements changed?
- Which challenge best helped you answer your guiding question?
- What did your checks establish, and what have you not checked yet?

- What does this test prove, and what could still be broken?
- Which edge cases and invalid inputs did you test?
- Can you make this test fail on purpose? Is the assertion strict enough?
- How do you test the component's state without checking the browser by hand?
- What does the coverage report not tell you?

<details>
<summary>Asynchronous flow and state (competency 4)</summary>

- What happens while the request is in progress, and when it fails?
- What happens if two responses arrive out of order?
- Where does the state live, and what is allowed to change it?
- What happens after a refresh, or when stored data is missing or malformed?
- Trace one user interaction from the event to the state store.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Accessibility (competency 5)</summary>

- How does a keyboard user operate this, and where does focus go?
- Which ARIA attributes change as the component changes, and why?
- What does the accessibility tree show for this element?
- What would an automated tool miss here, and how did you check it?
- How is a dynamic update announced?

</details>

<details>
<summary>AI oversight (competency 6)</summary>

- Which AI suggestion did you reject, and what evidence did you use?
- How did the wording of your prompt change the result?
- Which part of the AI's output was wrong or outdated?
- How would you review this AI-generated change in a pull request?
- How can someone else follow your AI audit trail?

</details>

<summary>Debugging and AI use</summary>

- What was the original symptom, and what did you expect instead?
- What evidence led to your root-cause hypothesis?
- Which causes did you rule out?
- How did you verify the fix and check for regressions?
- If you used AI, what did it suggest, what did you reject, and how did you verify the result?

</details>

## Tips

**Explaining:** start with the goal before opening the code, use real examples, and say what you do not know. A clear explanation of one slice beats a rushed tour of the whole project.

**Reviewing:** ask to understand, not to prove you know more. Give your partner time to think. Point to specific evidence when giving feedback, and end with one strength and one suggested next step.

## You are done when

- every slice in the contract has been discussed,
- you have evidence or a clear explanation for each slice,
- both partners have asked and answered questions,
- gaps and next steps are recorded, and
- your Competency Progress Tracker is updated.

When every slice of a competency has been reviewed, request the [competency pass-off](../competency-pass-off-new).
